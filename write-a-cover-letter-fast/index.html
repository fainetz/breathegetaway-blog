How to write a cover letter fast (that still sounds like you)

November 18, 2026

Short answer: To write a cover letter fast, use three short paragraphs: why this role at this company, two specific results from your past that match the job, and a one-line close. Keep it under a page, reuse the structure for every application, and only rewrite the parts that name the company and the matching results.

How long should a cover letter be?

Under one page, usually three or four short paragraphs. Hiring managers skim. A tight letter that hits the job's main requirements gets read; a long one gets skipped.

What goes in each paragraph?

Opening: the role you're applying for and one real reason you want it at this company. Middle: two results from your past that match what the posting asks for, with numbers if you have them. Close: a line saying you'd like to talk, and thanks.

How do you tailor it quickly?

Keep a base letter. For each job, swap in the company name, the role, one line about why that company, and the two results that best match the posting's top requirements. That's a few minutes per application instead of starting over.

How do you use AI without sounding generic?

Give the AI the job posting and your resume, and ask for a draft using your real results. Then cut anything you wouldn't say out loud, like "I am thrilled to apply" or "dynamic team player." Specific beats polished.

Drafting it from the posting on your screen

Utterseen is a free AI assistant for Mac and Windows that sees your screen. With a job posting open, hold a key and ask for a cover letter for this job; it drafts one from your resume and can paste it into the application form. It can also tailor your resume to the same posting and fill out the rest of the form.

Questions people ask

Do employers still read cover letters?

Some do and some don't, and you usually can't tell which. A short, specific letter is worth sending when the application allows one, especially for roles you really want.

Is it okay to use AI to write a cover letter?

Yes, as long as the facts are true and you edit it to sound like you. Remove generic phrases and make sure the results it mentions are your real ones.

Should I write a new cover letter for every job?

Tailor it, don't rewrite it. Keep the structure and swap the company, role and the results that match each posting.
