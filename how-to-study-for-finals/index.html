How to study for finals in less time

November 18, 2026

Short answer: To study for finals efficiently, start about a week out, spend most of your time quizzing yourself on practice questions instead of re-reading, spread reviews across several days, and protect your sleep the night before. Use AI to make the practice questions and study guides so your time goes into recall.

When should you start studying for finals?

About a week before each exam, in short daily sessions. Spreading the same hours over several days helps you remember more than one long cram session, which is one of the most consistent findings in learning research.

What should you actually do in each session?

Quiz yourself. Work old exams and practice problems, or have AI write questions from your notes, and answer them without looking. Mark what you miss and start the next session with those. Re-reading and highlighting feel productive but help much less.

How do you cover a whole semester fast?

Make a one-page study guide per unit: key terms, formulas and the kinds of questions your teacher asks. AI can draft it from your slides and notes in minutes, and you fix what's off. Then quiz from the guide every day.

Does sleep really matter the night before?

Yes. Sleep helps your brain hold onto what you studied, and an all-nighter makes recall slower on test day. A shorter review in the evening and a full night's sleep beats studying until 4 a.m.

A study helper that sees your screen

Utterseen is a free AI voice assistant for Mac and Windows. With your slides, notes or a practice exam open, hold a key and ask it to quiz you, explain a problem step by step, or make a study guide PDF. It works on whatever's on your screen, so there's no copying into a chat window.

Questions people ask

How many hours a day should I study for finals?

There's no fixed number. Several focused sessions of 30 to 60 minutes with breaks, spread over a week, usually beat one long day of cramming.

Is it better to re-read notes or do practice problems?

Practice problems. Testing yourself is one of the best-supported study methods; re-reading is one of the weakest.

Can AI make a study guide from my notes?

Yes. Give it your notes or slides and ask for key terms, formulas and likely questions. Utterseen can turn whatever's on your screen into a study guide PDF.
