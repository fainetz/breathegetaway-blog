How to summarize a lecture or YouTube video in minutes

November 18, 2026

Short answer: To summarize a lecture or YouTube video fast, use the video's transcript: most YouTube videos have one, and an AI tool can turn it into a short summary with the main points and timestamps. Then watch only the parts you didn't understand and quiz yourself on the rest.

Start from the transcript

On YouTube, most videos have captions you can open as a transcript from the description area. A transcript lets an AI read an hour of talking in seconds. Recorded class videos on course sites often have captions too.

Ask for the right kind of summary

A one-paragraph summary is easy to forget. Ask for the five main ideas, the key terms with one-line definitions, and where in the video each idea comes up. That gives you a map of the lecture you can jump around in.

Watch the hard parts, quiz the rest

Use the summary to skip what you already know and rewatch only the confusing sections. Then have the AI ask you questions on the whole lecture. Answering without looking is what makes it stick.

Doing it without leaving the video

Utterseen is a free AI voice assistant for Mac and Windows that sees your screen. With a lecture or YouTube video open, hold a key and ask "summarize this" or "quiz me on this," and it answers out loud. It can also turn the lecture into a study guide PDF or Anki flashcards.

Questions people ask

Can I get a transcript of a YouTube video?

Yes, for most videos. Open the video, expand the description and choose Show transcript. Videos without captions have no transcript, so a tool would need to transcribe the audio.

Is it okay to skip parts of a lecture?

Skipping what you already understand is fine as long as you check yourself with questions afterward. Use a summary to decide which parts need a full watch.

What's the fastest way to study from a video?

Get a structured summary, rewatch only the confusing parts, then quiz yourself. Utterseen does all three from your screen.
